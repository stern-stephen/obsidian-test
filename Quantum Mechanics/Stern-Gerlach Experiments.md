# Stern-Gerlach Experiments

## Overview

**Stern-Gerlach experiments** use an inhomogeneous magnetic field to separate particles according to a component of angular momentum.

For spin-$1/2$ particles, a Stern-Gerlach analyzer oriented along the $z$ axis has two possible output channels:

$$
S_z = +\frac{\hbar}{2}
$$

and:

$$
S_z = -\frac{\hbar}{2}
$$

These outcomes are represented by the states:

$$
|+z\rangle,\quad |-z\rangle
$$

## Intuition

A Stern-Gerlach apparatus is both a measurement device and a state-preparation device.

If the $|+z\rangle$ output channel is selected, the outgoing particles are prepared in the state $|+z\rangle$. A second $S_z$ measurement gives the same result with certainty.

If an $S_x$ analyzer is used after preparing $|+z\rangle$, the beam splits into two channels. This shows that a definite $z$-component of spin does not imply a definite $x$-component of spin.

## Sequential Measurements

Sequential Stern-Gerlach experiments are important because they show that quantum measurement is not just passive sorting.

A measurement along one axis can change what will happen in a later measurement along another axis. This behavior is central to [spin-1/2 systems](Spin-1-2%20Systems.md) and to the idea of incompatible observables.
