# Symmetries

## Overview

A symmetry is a transformation that leaves the physical content of a system unchanged.

In mechanics, the deepest use of symmetry is that continuous symmetries lead to conserved quantities. This is the classical idea behind Noether's theorem.

$$
\text{continuous symmetry} \rightarrow \text{conserved quantity}
$$

## Definition

A transformation is a symmetry when applying it does not change the equations of motion or the Hamiltonian structure of the system.

Examples:

- Spatial translations preserve the physics of a system with no preferred location.
- Rotations preserve the physics of a system with no preferred direction.
- Time translations preserve the physics of a system with no explicit time dependence.

## Generators

In Hamiltonian mechanics, transformations are generated by phase-space functions.

If $G(q,p)$ generates a small transformation with parameter $\epsilon$, then any phase-space function $f(q,p)$ changes by:

$$
\delta f=\epsilon \lbrace f,G\rbrace
$$

So the generator has two roles:

- It is a physical quantity.
- It produces the corresponding infinitesimal transformation through the Poisson bracket.

## Symmetry And Conservation

In Lagrangian mechanics, if a transformation parameter is an independent cyclic coordinate $q_j$, then:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_j}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_j}\right)=0
$$

Translations make the associated linear momentum component the conserved conjugate momentum. Rotations make the corresponding angular momentum component the conserved conjugate momentum.

In Hamiltonian mechanics, the same connection appears through generators.

If $G$ generates a symmetry of the Hamiltonian, then:

$$
\delta H=\epsilon \lbrace H,G\rbrace=0
$$

This implies:

$$
\lbrace G,H\rbrace=0
$$

If $G$ has no explicit time dependence, then:

$$
\frac{dG}{dt}=\lbrace G,H\rbrace=0
$$

So $G$ is conserved.

## Key Examples

| Symmetry | Generator | Conserved quantity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spatial translation | Momentum | Linear momentum |
| Rotation | Angular momentum | Angular momentum |
| Time translation | Hamiltonian | Energy |

## Quantum Bridge

In quantum mechanics, Poisson brackets are replaced structurally by commutators:

$$
\lbrace f,g\rbrace \leftrightarrow \frac{1}{i\hbar}[F,G]
$$

The Hamiltonian still generates time evolution, and conserved observables commute with the Hamiltonian when they have no explicit time dependence:

$$
[G,H]=0
$$

## Common Confusions

- A symmetry is not just a pretty shape; it is invariance of the dynamics.
- A transformation can be mathematically possible without being a symmetry of a particular system.
- Conservation follows from continuous symmetries, not from arbitrary relabeling.
- A conserved quantity can also be the generator of the symmetry that protects it.
