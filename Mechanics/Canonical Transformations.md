# Canonical Transformations

## Overview

Canonical transformations are changes of phase-space variables that preserve the Hamiltonian form of the equations of motion.

## Definition

A transformation from $(q,p)$ to $(Q,P)$ is canonical when the new variables obey Hamilton's equations with the transformed Hamiltonian.

Equivalently, canonical variables preserve the basic Poisson bracket structure:

$$
\{Q_i,P_j\} = \delta_{ij}
$$

## Intuition

Canonical transformations are the phase-space analogue of choosing better coordinates while preserving the mechanics.

## Why It Matters

They reveal which features of Hamiltonian mechanics are coordinate-independent and prepare the idea that transformations can be generated by conserved quantities.
