# Equivalence Principle

## Overview

The equivalence principle says that, in a sufficiently small local experiment, the effects of a uniform gravitational field can be indistinguishable from the effects of an accelerated reference frame.

It does not say that every kind of motion is experimentally indistinguishable in every sense. Acceleration, rotation, and gravitational curvature can have measurable local or extended effects. The point is more precise: physics should not depend on an absolute background frame, and a local observer may not be able to assign a unique split between gravity, frame acceleration, and inertial-force bookkeeping.

## Local Versus Global Tests

The word "local" matters. In a small closed room, standing on a floor in a uniform gravitational field can feel the same as standing on the floor of a rocket accelerating through gravity-free space. The same contact pressure may be explained by different frame choices.

But larger or more sensitive experiments can distinguish situations that are only locally equivalent:

- A nonuniform gravitational field produces tidal effects: nearby freely falling bodies accelerate relative to one another.
- A rotating frame produces centrifugal and Coriolis effects.
- A nonuniformly accelerating frame can produce position-dependent inertial effects.

Those effects are not made unreal by calling them frame-dependent. They are observable features of the chosen local dynamics and constraints.

## Relation To General Relativity

General relativity removes the need for a preferred inertial frame by writing the laws in a coordinate-independent way. This is not the same claim as "all frames feel identical." Instead, any coordinate frame can be used if the metric, connection, inertial terms, and forces are transformed consistently.

At a single event, or within a sufficiently small freely falling laboratory, gravitational effects can be transformed away to first order. Curvature and tidal effects cannot be transformed away over a finite region.

## Common Confusions

- "No preferred frame" does not mean "no detectable acceleration."
- "Frame-dependent" does not mean "physically fake."
- The equivalence between gravity and acceleration is local and approximate when tidal effects are negligible.
- A contact force, such as a floor or chair pushing on you, can be the immediate thing your body feels even when the same situation is described using gravity or inertial forces.
