## Big Ideas

- Canonical transformations are phase-space transformations that preserve the form of Hamilton's equations.
- Generating functions provide a systematic way to construct canonical transformations.
- Some canonical transformations can simplify a Hamiltonian enough that integration becomes trivial.
- [Poisson brackets](../../../Mechanics/Poisson%20Brackets.md) test canonical structure and express equations of motion.
- Infinitesimal canonical transformations are generated by phase-space functions.
- The Hamiltonian generates time evolution.
- Constants of motion generate symmetries of the Hamiltonian.
- Phase-space volume is invariant under canonical transformations, leading to Liouville's theorem.
- Angular momentum generates rotations, and its Poisson-bracket algebra anticipates the quantum angular-momentum commutator algebra.

## Logical Progression

1. Motivate canonical transformations as a route to integrating Hamilton's equations.
2. Define transformations from $(q,p)$ to $(Q,P)$ that preserve Hamilton's form.
3. Use modified Hamilton's principle to derive generating-function rules.
4. Work through a harmonic-oscillator transformation where the new coordinate is cyclic.
5. Define Poisson brackets and their algebraic identities.
6. Rewrite time evolution as $du/dt=\lbrace u,H\rbrace+\partial u/\partial t$.
7. Interpret infinitesimal canonical transformations as generated flows.
8. Prove canonical invariance of phase-space volume and state Liouville's theorem.
9. Use angular momentum as the generator of rotations.
