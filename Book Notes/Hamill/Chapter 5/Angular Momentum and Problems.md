## Purpose

Hamill uses angular momentum as the main physical example of infinitesimal canonical transformations. The result is a classical Poisson-bracket algebra that closely resembles quantum angular momentum.

## 5.4.4 Angular Momentum As A Generator

If a system is symmetric under rotations, the rotation angle is cyclic and the conjugate generalized momentum is angular momentum.

Conversely, angular momentum generates rotations. For the $z$ component:

$$
L_z=\sum_i(x_i p_{y_i}-y_i p_{x_i})
$$

Using $G=L_z$ in the infinitesimal transformation gives the ordinary infinitesimal rotation formulas for both positions and momenta.

This is the pattern:

- momentum generates translations,
- angular momentum generates rotations,
- the Hamiltonian generates time evolution.

## 5.5 Angular Momentum In Poisson Brackets

For a system vector $\mathbf F$, an infinitesimal rotation about unit vector $\mathbf n$ generated by $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf L$ gives:

$$
\lbrace \mathbf F,\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf L\rbrace=\mathbf n\times\mathbf F
$$

In components:

$$
\lbrace F_i,L_j\rbrace=\epsilon_{ijk}F_k
$$

Taking $\mathbf F=\mathbf L$ gives:

$$
\lbrace L_i,L_j\rbrace=\epsilon_{ijk}L_k
$$

This means different angular-momentum components are not mutually canonical momenta. No two components of $\mathbf L$ can simultaneously be treated as canonical variables.

However:

$$
\lbrace L^2,\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf L\rbrace=0
$$

so $L^2$ and one component of $\mathbf L$ are compatible. Hamill explicitly points out the quantum echo: in quantum mechanics, $L^2$ and one angular-momentum component can be quantized together, but distinct components cannot all have sharp values simultaneously.

## Poisson's Theorem

Hamill states Poisson's theorem: the Poisson bracket of two constants of motion is also a constant of motion.

This gives a way to generate new conserved quantities from known ones. For example, if suitable components of angular and linear momentum are conserved, their brackets can imply conservation of additional components.
