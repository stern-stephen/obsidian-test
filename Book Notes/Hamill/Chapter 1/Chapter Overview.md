## Big Ideas

- Analytical mechanics begins by replacing Cartesian component bookkeeping with coordinates adapted to the system.
- Generalized coordinates need not be distances or vector components; they are any independent parameters that specify configuration.
- Constraints reduce degrees of freedom and motivate choosing coordinates that already respect the allowed motion.
- Virtual displacements are fixed-time, constraint-compatible comparison displacements.
- Generalized forces are defined by the coefficients of the generalized-coordinate variations in virtual work.
- Configuration space represents the whole system by one point; phase space adds conjugate momenta.
- Hamill presents Lagrange's equations as a working tool before deriving them from first principles later.
- Cyclic coordinates connect symmetry to conserved generalized momentum.
- Time-translation symmetry leads to conservation of the energy function under suitable conditions.

## Logical Progression

1. Review position, velocity, acceleration, and coordinate transformations.
2. Introduce generalized coordinates as independent parameters adapted to constraints.
3. Relate generalized velocities to Cartesian velocities through the chain rule.
4. Use constraints to count degrees of freedom and restrict virtual displacements.
5. Define generalized force through virtual work.
6. Separate configuration space from phase space.
7. Use the Lagrangian as a practical equation-generating device.
