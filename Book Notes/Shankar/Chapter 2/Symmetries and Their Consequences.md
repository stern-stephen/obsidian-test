## Big Ideas

- Symmetries connect transformations with conserved quantities.
- Spatial translation symmetry is tied to conservation of momentum.
- Rotational symmetry is tied to conservation of angular momentum.
- Time-translation symmetry is tied to conservation of energy.
- This classical story prepares the quantum story where symmetries are represented by operators.

## Notes

Shankar ends the classical review with symmetry because it unifies transformations, generators, and conservation laws in the same Poisson-bracket language that will later become operator and commutator language.

His key distinction is between a mere relabeling and a physical invariance. A translation is a symmetry for a free particle but not generally for a particle in a position-dependent external potential. When a phase-space function generates a continuous transformation that leaves the Hamiltonian invariant, that generator is conserved.

The full generator equations, conservation proof, and symmetry table live in [Symmetries](../../../Mechanics/Symmetries.md). The general conserved-quantity conditions are collected in [Conservation Laws](../../../Mechanics/Conservation%20Laws.md).

## Core Correspondences

- Spatial translation symmetry gives momentum conservation.
- Rotational symmetry gives angular momentum conservation.
- Time-translation symmetry gives energy conservation.

## Quantum Bridge

The chapter closes by making its intended bridge explicit: phase-space functions become operators, Poisson brackets become commutators, and canonical transformations become unitary transformations. Momentum generating translations is Shankar's prototype for this classical-to-quantum correspondence.

## Common Confusions

- A symmetry is not just visual prettiness; it is an invariance of the equations or action.
- Conservation laws follow from continuous symmetries, not from every possible relabeling.
- A generator is both an observable and a machine for producing a transformation.
- If a transformation changes the Hamiltonian, it is not a symmetry of that system, even if the transformation is mathematically allowed.
- The quantum commutator analogy is structural, but Poisson brackets and commutators live in different mathematical settings.
