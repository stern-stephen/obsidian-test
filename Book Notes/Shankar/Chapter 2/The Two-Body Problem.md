## Big Ideas

- A two-particle problem with a central interaction can be reduced to center-of-mass motion plus relative motion.
- The relative coordinate behaves like a one-body problem with the reduced mass.
- This reduction is reused in quantum mechanics, especially for hydrogen-like systems.

## Notes

Shankar includes the two-body reduction because the same change to center-of-mass and relative coordinates will reappear in quantum systems such as hydrogen. For an interaction depending only on separation, the overall motion decouples from an effective one-body problem with reduced mass.

The coordinate definitions and reduced-mass formula are maintained in [Two-Body Problem](../../../Mechanics/Two-Body%20Problem.md). The durable lesson is that this separation is an exact change of variables under the stated interaction assumption, not an approximation.

## Common Confusions

- The reduced mass is not a new physical particle. It is an effective mass for the relative coordinate.
- The center-of-mass coordinate and relative coordinate are a change of variables, not an approximation.
- The separation works cleanly when the interaction depends only on the relative separation.
