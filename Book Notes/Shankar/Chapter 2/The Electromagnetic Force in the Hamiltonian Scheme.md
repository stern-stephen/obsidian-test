## Big Ideas

- Electromagnetic coupling has a clean Hamiltonian form when canonical and mechanical momentum are kept distinct.
- The combination $\mathbf{p} - q\mathbf{A}$ is the mechanical momentum.
- The same substitution becomes minimal coupling in quantum mechanics.

## Notes

For a charged particle in electromagnetic potentials, the Hamiltonian can be written:

$$
H = \frac{1}{2m}(\mathbf{p} - q\mathbf{A})^2 + q\phi
$$

Here $\mathbf{p}$ is canonical momentum. The velocity is related to:

$$
m\dot{\mathbf{r}} = \mathbf{p} - q\mathbf{A}
$$

This is the Hamiltonian version of the same physics encoded in the electromagnetic Lagrangian.

## Quantum Bridge

The classical expression suggests the quantum replacement:

$$
\mathbf{p} \to -i\hbar\nabla
$$

with electromagnetic coupling introduced through:

$$
-i\hbar\nabla \to -i\hbar\nabla - q\mathbf{A}
$$

## Common Confusions

- The canonical momentum is the phase-space coordinate; the mechanical momentum is tied directly to velocity.
- The vector potential appears in the Hamiltonian even though the magnetic force depends on velocity crossed with magnetic field.
- Minimal coupling is not an arbitrary quantum trick; it is already visible in the classical Hamiltonian.
