## Big Ideas

- Chapter 3 is Shankar's short physical motivation for quantum mechanics.
- Classical physics separates particles and waves cleanly, but microscopic experiments do not respect that separation.
- The double-slit experiment shows why definite classical trajectories cannot be the whole story.
- Light and matter both force a mixed lesson: localized detections occur, but probabilities are governed by interference.
- The chapter prepares the meaning of a quantum state as a wavefunction or ket whose squared amplitude predicts detection probabilities.
