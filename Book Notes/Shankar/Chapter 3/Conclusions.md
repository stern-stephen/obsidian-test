## Notes

Shankar's conclusion is that microscopic entities combine features that classical mechanics keeps separate.

When detected, an electron or photon appears localized. But between preparation and detection, the successful predictive object is a wavefunction-like amplitude. Its modulus squared gives a probability density, not an ordinary material density.

In position language:

$$
P(x) \propto |\psi(x,t)|^2
$$

In the vector-space language from Chapter 1, the same state can be represented as a ket:

$$
|\psi(t)\rangle
$$

The next chapter turns this motivation into postulates: what counts as a state, how observables are represented, how measurement works, and how states evolve.
