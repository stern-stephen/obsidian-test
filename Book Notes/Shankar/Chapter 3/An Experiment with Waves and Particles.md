## Notes

The two-slit setup is a clean way to separate particle-like and wave-like predictions.

For classical particles, opening both slits gives the sum of the one-slit patterns. Each particle is assumed to pass through one slit or the other, so the total intensity has no interference term.

For classical waves, the disturbances from the two slits add as amplitudes before intensities are computed. This produces bright and dark fringes. The difference is the cross term that comes from superposing amplitudes.

## Key Equation

For waves, the intensity is not usually the sum of the separate intensities:

$$
I_{12}=|\psi_1+\psi_2|^2
$$

The interference term is what classical particles do not have.

## Why It Matters

This experiment gives a diagnostic: if a system gives localized detections but also an interference pattern, neither simple classical category is adequate.
