## Big Ideas

- D'Alembert's principle turns dynamics into a virtual-work statement by adding inertial forces to applied forces.
- The inertial force is the reversed mass-acceleration term.
- The principle generalizes the statics principle of virtual work to moving systems.
- Coopersmith treats so-called fictitious forces as real inertial effects associated with the chosen frame and constraints.
- Conservation of energy follows from D'Alembert's principle only under special assumptions such as time-independent external conditions.

2. Introduce reversed mass-acceleration as inertial force.
3. Apply the method to a simple moving system.
4. Explain why D'Alembert's principle is not just Newton's law rewritten.
5. Use accelerated reference frames to reinterpret fictitious forces.
6. Derive energy conservation from D'Alembert's principle under restrictive assumptions.
7. Summarize the principle as the zero virtual work of dynamical forces.
