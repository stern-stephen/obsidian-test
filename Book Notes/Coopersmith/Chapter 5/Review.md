## Big Ideas

- D'Alembert's principle treats dynamics as statics plus inertial forces.
- Inertial forces are reactive forces, like constraint forces, but associated with reversed accelerations.
- Coopersmith sees the principle as deeper than Newtonian mechanics because it adds postulates about virtual work and reactive forces.
- The most compact form is that the total virtual work of the dynamical forces vanishes.

## Summary Statement

Coopersmith's review can be condensed into this condition:

$$
\sum_i\delta\omega_i^{dyn}=0
$$

The dynamical virtual work includes applied forces and inertial forces, while ideal constraint forces are removed by compatible virtual displacements.

## Why It Matters

The chapter prepares the transition to Lagrangian mechanics. Once dynamics has been expressed as a virtual-work balance, it becomes natural to replace particle-by-particle force equations with generalized coordinates and variational equations.
