## Ideal Fluids

Variational methods apply naturally to ideal fluids, including gases, when viscosity can be neglected. Viscosity introduces frictional dissipation, which is not easily incorporated into the simple conservative action formulation discussed here.

## Kinematic Conditions Replace Unknown Forces

The advantage of the variational approach is familiar from constrained mechanics: one need not calculate every internal force enforcing the allowed motion. For an incompressible fluid, for example, the fixed-volume condition can be imposed directly rather than deriving the molecular forces that resist compression.

The method therefore distinguishes two roles:

- dynamics is encoded in the action;
- admissible motion is restricted by kinematic constraints.
