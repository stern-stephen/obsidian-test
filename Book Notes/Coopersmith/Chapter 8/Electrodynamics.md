## Generalized Energies

Electrodynamics requires a more general Lagrangian than elementary mechanics. The interaction term for a charged particle in electromagnetic fields depends on velocity, and relativistic kinetic energy is not simply a fixed mass times speed squared.

The action principle itself does not fail. The theory must supply a Lagrangian with the correct covariant form so every inertial observer describes the same physics.

## Lenz's Law

Lenz's law says an induced current has the direction that opposes the change producing it. The familiar example is a magnet falling slowly through a conducting copper pipe because induced currents create magnetic fields that resist the magnet's motion.

Coopersmith interprets this coordinated microscopic response as part of the larger action-based dynamics. Lenz's law describes the direction locally, while the electromagnetic action gives the unified dynamical account.
