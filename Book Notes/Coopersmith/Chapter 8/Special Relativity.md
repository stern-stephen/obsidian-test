## From Absolute Light Speed To Spacetime

Special relativity treats the vacuum speed of light, $c$, as invariant. Space and time separately must therefore depend on the observer, while the spacetime interval between events remains invariant.

Proper time, $\tau$, is the time measured by a clock traveling along the worldline. Although observers can disagree about spatial and temporal coordinate differences, they can calculate the same proper time for that worldline.

## Relativistic Free-Particle Action

For a free, uncharged particle of rest mass $m_0$, Coopersmith gives the relativistic Lagrangian as

$$
L=-m_0c^2\frac{d\tau}{dt}.
$$

The action is therefore

$$
A=-m_0c^2\int d\tau.
$$

Because the multiplying factor is negative, minimizing the action corresponds to maximizing the elapsed proper time. Coopersmith calls this the Principle of Maximum Ageing. More precisely, the physical timelike worldline makes proper time stationary and, for the nearby free trajectories considered here, maximal.

## Why The Rest Frame Is Not An Absolute Frame

Using the particle's rest frame does not restore Newtonian absolute space. The rest frame is selected by the specific worldline, not privileged for all physics. Other inertial observers can transform their measurements and agree on the proper time along that path.
