## Gravity As Geometry

Special relativity uses flat spacetime. General relativity allows energy and momentum to curve spacetime. A freely falling body then follows a timelike geodesic: a path of stationary, locally maximal proper time between nearby events.

This replaces the Newtonian picture of a gravitational force with a geometric picture. The body is not deflected from force-free motion; its geodesic is the straightest available motion in curved spacetime.

## Orbital Proper Time

Coopersmith illustrates the balance using a clock in orbit. At a smaller orbital radius the clock moves faster and lies deeper in the gravitational field, while at a larger radius it moves more slowly and experiences less gravitational time dilation. The physical free orbit makes the combined proper time stationary.

## Local Frames And Global Spacetime

The variational comparison is local. A large curved region is treated as a patchwork of sufficiently small local frames, stitched together consistently. Stronger curvature requires smaller patches. This preserves the local validity of free-fall reasoning without pretending that one inertial frame covers all of spacetime.

## Matter As The Source Of Curvature

General relativity is complete in the sense that it describes both how bodies move in a given geometry and how matter generates that geometry. The source is not mass alone. Energy density, momentum density, and stress are collected in the stress-energy tensor.
