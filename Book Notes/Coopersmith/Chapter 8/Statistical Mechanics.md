## Why Exact Trajectories Are Abandoned

A macroscopic sample contains so many degrees of freedom that its complete microscopic state cannot be measured or followed. Statistical mechanics therefore does not try to predict one exact molecular trajectory from perfect initial data.

## Ensembles In Phase Space

Instead, one considers an ensemble of hypothetical systems prepared under the same macroscopic conditions but occupying different microscopic states. Each possible state corresponds to a point in phase space. A distribution over those points represents the available possibilities.

Predictions about the actual system are then statistical: they concern expected or average behavior derived from the ensemble rather than a fully specified microscopic history.

## Why Hamilton's Extra Coordinates Are Affordable

Hamiltonian mechanics doubles the coordinate description by using both generalized positions and conjugate momenta. For a system already containing an enormous number of variables, this factor of two is not the main difficulty. The useful phase-space structure more than compensates for the formal increase.
