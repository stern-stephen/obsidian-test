## Chapter Focus

Chapter 8 surveys how variational mechanics reaches beyond its classical-mechanics origins. Coopersmith's central claim is that the same action-based framework survives when the physical interpretation changes: least time in optics, maximum proper time in relativity, covariant Lagrangians in electrodynamics, constrained ideal flows in hydrodynamics, phase-space ensembles in statistical mechanics, and interference among histories in quantum theory.

The chapter is qualitative rather than a derivation. Its purpose is to show that action provides a common language across theories whose forces, coordinates, and governing equations look very different.

## Big Ideas

- Variational mechanics separates the universal optimization rule from the theory-specific choice of action.
- In classical mechanics, a system makes the action built from $L=T-V$ stationary.
- In relativity, the free-particle action turns stationary action into maximum proper time.
- In general relativity, free fall becomes geodesic motion in curved spacetime rather than motion caused by a gravitational force.
- Phase space remains useful when exact trajectories give way to statistical ensembles.
- Planck's constant has units of action and marks the scale at which quantum behavior becomes unavoidable.
- In Feynman's formulation, all paths contribute amplitudes; the classical path survives because nearby phases reinforce one another around stationary action.

## Chapter Through-Line

The unifying feature is not a single formula such as $L=T-V$. Each field supplies its own physically appropriate Lagrangian or action. What persists is the variational structure: compare nearby possible histories and identify the history, or dominant family of histories, whose action is stationary.
