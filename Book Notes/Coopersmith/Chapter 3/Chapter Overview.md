## Big Ideas

- Coopersmith prepares the mathematical language needed for variational mechanics.
- Generalized coordinates replace Newton's fixed three-dimensional background space with coordinates tailored to the system.
- Degrees of freedom are independent characteristics of a system, not merely a feature of one coordinate description.
- Configuration space is the abstract testing ground in which a whole system becomes one point.
- The calculus of variations extends ordinary stationary-point tests from numbers to whole functions or paths.

## Logical Progression

1. Contrast Newton's fixed space with Lagrange's system-dependent coordinates.
2. Treat time as a special coordinate-like parameter in dynamics.
3. Count independent coordinates after constraints are accounted for.
4. Replace force-centered mechanics with energy and generalized work.
5. Introduce configuration space as an abstract space of system states.
6. Use invariants to separate physical facts from coordinate descriptions.
7. Move from ordinary extrema to stationary integrals.
