## Big Ideas

- Coopersmith presents Hamilton's optics-mechanics analogy as a historical route toward wave-particle duality.
- Huygens's wavefront construction has a mechanical analogue using surfaces of common action.
- De Broglie's relation links momentum with wavelength.
- The Hamilton-Jacobi equation appears as a short-wavelength or classical limit of the later wave-mechanics picture.
- Schrodinger's equation becomes the wave equation whose classical limit is related to Hamilton-Jacobi mechanics.

## Huygens Construction

In optics, a wavefront advances by treating each point on the old wavefront as the source of secondary wavelets. The new wavefront is the envelope of those wavelets.

Coopersmith applies the same picture to mechanics. A surface of common action advances by sending short mechanical rays normal to the old action surface and joining their tips to form the new surface.

## De Broglie Relation

The quantum bridge comes from treating particles as having a wavelength related to momentum:

$$
p=\frac{h}{\lambda}
$$

This makes the mechanical momentum in Hamiltonian theory look like the wave-vector information in wave theory.

## Classical Limit

Coopersmith's point is not that Hamiltonian mechanics alone proves quantum mechanics. The historical and mathematical point is that the Hamilton-Jacobi equation already has a wavefront structure. In the short-wavelength limit, wave mechanics reduces toward Hamilton-Jacobi mechanics, just as geometrical optics is the short-wavelength limit of wave optics.
