## Chapter Focus

Chapter 7 develops Hamiltonian mechanics as a reformulation of Lagrangian mechanics using paired coordinates and momenta. Coopersmith presents Hamilton's move as "asking less from more": double the coordinate description from $q_i$ to $(q_i,p_i)$, then use the resulting phase-space structure to expose qualitative information, conservation laws, and the bridge to wave mechanics.

The central equations are Hamilton's canonical equations:

$$
\dot{q}_i=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}
$$

$$
\dot{p}_i=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_i}
$$

## Big Ideas

- Hamiltonian mechanics replaces generalized velocities with conjugate momenta.
- The new variables live in phase space, where each point represents both configuration and momentum.
- The canonical equations split motion into first-order equations for $q_i$ and $p_i$.
- Phase-space flow gives qualitative information even when exact equations of motion are hard to solve.
- Conservation laws become especially transparent in Hamiltonian form.
- The Hamilton-Jacobi equation links Hamiltonian mechanics, action surfaces, optics, and the later quantum wave picture.
