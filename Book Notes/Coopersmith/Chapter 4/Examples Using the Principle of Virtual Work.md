## Big Ideas

- The examples show how virtual work solves equilibrium problems without opening up hidden internal mechanisms.
- Sign conventions matter: virtual work is positive when force and virtual displacement point together and negative when they oppose.
- Constraint equations relate the virtual displacements before the virtual-work sum is evaluated.
- Actual energy changes over finite motion must not be confused with virtual work about the equilibrium point.

## Black Box

Two pushrods are connected through hidden reversible mechanisms. If rod A moves inward while rod B moves outward by a fixed ratio, the virtual-work condition relates the two external forces without requiring the internal transmission details.

The key lesson is that Newtonian force analysis would require the box to be opened, but virtual work only needs the input-output displacement relation.

## Knob And Slider

An angular virtual displacement of a knob is linked to a linear virtual displacement of a slider. The generalized virtual-work statement is:

$$
M\delta\theta+F\delta x=0
$$

Here the torque-like quantity $M$ and the force $F$ pair with different kinds of virtual displacement, but both products are energies.

## Ladder

For a ladder leaning against a wall, the length constraint relates the vertical displacement of the top to the horizontal displacement of the bottom. The wall and floor normal reactions do no virtual work because the allowed virtual displacements run along the surfaces.

The static friction force at the floor is included because it acts along the allowed virtual displacement.

## Springs, Bubbles, And Capacitors

The spring examples distinguish virtual displacement from actual relaxation to equilibrium. A finite compression path may involve oscillation and dissipation, while virtual work tests the neighborhood of the final equilibrium point.

The soap bubble example balances pressure work against surface-tension work.

The capacitor example uses the virtual change in stored electrical energy to infer the force between plates. Coopersmith uses it to show that virtual work can bypass detailed microscopic force distributions.
