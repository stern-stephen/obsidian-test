## Big Ideas

- Goldstein begins with Newtonian particle mechanics and builds toward the Lagrangian formulation.
- Conservation laws for particle systems depend on specific assumptions about external and internal forces.
- Constraints reduce the independent degrees of freedom but introduce unknown constraint forces.
- Generalized coordinates incorporate holonomic constraints directly into the description of configuration.
- D'Alembert's principle removes ideal constraint forces by considering virtual displacements.
- Lagrange's equations replace vector force balances with scalar functions and independent coordinates.
- Velocity-dependent potentials and Rayleigh's dissipation function extend the method beyond ordinary conservative forces.

## Logical Progression

1. State Newton's laws and the particle conservation theorems.
2. Extend momentum, angular momentum, and energy to many-particle systems.
3. Classify constraints and count the true degrees of freedom.
4. Use virtual work and D'Alembert's principle to eliminate ideal constraint forces.
5. Derive Lagrange's equations in generalized coordinates.
6. Extend the formalism to electromagnetic and linearly dissipative forces.
7. Apply the method to Cartesian motion, polar motion, Atwood's machine, and a rotating wire.
