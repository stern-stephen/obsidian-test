## Big Ideas

- Hamilton's principle describes an entire trajectory in configuration space by requiring the action to be stationary under fixed-endpoint variations.
- The Euler-Lagrange equations are the local differential conditions implied by this global variational statement.
- Stationary does not necessarily mean minimum; distinguishing minima, maxima, and other stationary paths requires more than the first variation.
- Lagrange multipliers allow constraints to remain explicit and can recover the corresponding generalized constraint forces.
- The action formulation is independent of the coordinates used and extends naturally to fields, circuits, and other systems with the same mathematical structure.
- A cyclic coordinate signals a continuous invariance and has a conserved conjugate momentum.
- If the Lagrangian has no explicit time dependence, the energy function $h=\sum_j\dot q_jp_j-L$ is conserved, though it equals total energy only under additional conditions.

## Logical Progression

1. Replace an instantaneous virtual-work principle by a statement about complete paths.
2. Develop the variational machinery needed to find stationary functionals.
3. Apply that machinery to the action and recover Lagrange's equations.
4. Retain dependent coordinates by enforcing constraints with multipliers.
5. Use the coordinate-invariant structure to recognize analogies beyond particle mechanics.
6. Read conservation laws directly from absent coordinates and system symmetries.
7. Treat time in the same spirit and derive the conserved energy function.
